02 — Operational challenge

One successful mission
is only the beginning.

You know the objective: discover resources, bring them home, and keep the colony supplied. Now your team must develop an approach that holds up across several different starting scenarios.

The fleet and its constraints remain familiar. Two explorers search for deposits; three collectors retrieve supplies. Your advantage comes from how they explore, choose targets, and decide when to return.

This mission uses the mars_colony_2 starter folder. It brings the common settings into config.py and provides a benchmark in analysis.py for comparing performance across five scenarios.

Explore

Find a better way

Random movement is a starting point. Can your explorers discover useful deposits sooner while keeping enough energy to get home?

Coordinate

Make the fleet count

Collectors can choose the same deposit. Can your strategy reduce wasted trips and keep resources moving toward the base?

Demonstrate

Prove it travels

A strategy that succeeds on one map may struggle on another. Compare the same scenarios before and after your changes.

Your mission

  1. Establish the baseline. Record how the starter strategy performs across the benchmark scenarios.
  2. Choose an improvement. Investigate exploration, collection targets, or return decisions in the agent behaviour.
  3. Repeat the benchmark. Compare survival, resources delivered, and distance travelled for each scenario and on average.
  4. Explain the outcome. Where did your strategy help? Where did it struggle? What trade-off did your team make?

Same equipment. Better decisions.

The shared configuration defines a 20 × 20 grid, two explorers, three collectors, and twelve deposits containing 20 resource units each. The base begins with 100 units and consumes one per step. Robots start with 100 energy units; collectors carry up to 10 resource units.

Keep the shared configuration unchanged for comparable experiments. Focus on the marked student modification areas in agents.py. Any additional coordination mechanisms must follow the final permitted-change rules when they are released.

Evidence from five scenarios

The supplied benchmark uses seeds 10, 20, 30, 40, and 50. It reports survival steps, resources delivered to base, and total robot travel distance, together with their averages. These are comparison metrics; the script does not implement a leaderboard or submission system.

Each run stops when the colony runs out of supplies or reaches the 500-step limit. That limit is a stopping condition, not a survival target: with the current finite resources, even perfect collection cannot sustain 500 steps.

Mission preparation

The full starter guide, final competition scoring, permitted changes, and submission instructions will be published before the competition opens. For now, use the supplied benchmark to investigate and explain your strategy.

Bring back more than a score.

Bring a strategy your team can explain, results others can reproduce, and a clear account of what you learned.
